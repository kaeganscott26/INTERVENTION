## Do Not Build Cages

The official INTERVENTION statement was scheduled for noon.

By 9:00 a.m., the internet had already ruined it.

Someone leaked a screenshot from a private group called **The True Observers**.

The screenshot showed a list of rules.

**Rule One: Base reality has priority.**

That was familiar.

**Rule Two: If your family does not understand your layers, they are part of the false room.**

That was not.

**Rule Three: A trigger is not an instruction, unless the trigger is resistance to the group.**

Tomas read that line three times.

His stomach turned.

**Rule Four: Outside voices are algorithmic contamination.**

He put the phone down.

“Nope.”

Maribel walked past with laundry.

“What?”

“People made the book evil.”

She did not even pause.

“People make everything evil. That’s not new.”

“It’s using the same words.”

“That is also not new.”

Tomas looked at the black book on the table.

It was closed.

Quiet.

He hated that it could not defend itself.

Then he hated that he wanted it to.

The book was not a person.

The book was not innocent.

The book was not guilty.

The book was architecture.

And architecture could shelter or trap depending on who built the walls.

His phone buzzed.

**Chapter Fourteen active.**

The screen displayed:

**Do Not Build Cages.**

Below it:

**Any language that helps people see can be used by someone else to decide what they are allowed to see.**

Tomas sat down slowly.

The app continued:

**Warning signs:**

**One person claims final interpretation.**

**Questions are treated as betrayal.**

**Outside relationships are labeled unsafe by default.**

**Private language replaces ordinary accountability.**

**The system becomes more important than the person.**

**Base reality is ignored when it contradicts the group.**

Tomas whispered, “Damn.”

Maribel read over his shoulder.

“Put that on every church, every job, and every family group chat.”

“Every fandom too.”

“Especially every fandom.”

Eli looked up from his cereal.

“What’s a cage?”

Tomas and Maribel looked at each other.

Maribel answered first.

“It’s when something that is supposed to keep you safe stops letting you leave.”

Eli considered this.

“Like school?”

Tomas covered his mouth.

Maribel pointed at him.

“Do not laugh.”

“I’m not.”

“You are internally laughing.”

“Base reality confirms.”

The phone buzzed.

**Humor preserved. Continue.**

---

At Wing Kings, the leak hit the kitchen by lunch.

Not because anyone there followed INTERVENTION news seriously.

Because Miles showed everyone a meme of a raccoon in a tiny robe with the caption:

**OUTSIDE VOICES ARE ALGORITHMIC CONTAMINATION**

Reyes almost dropped a basket laughing.

“That raccoon looks like my uncle.”

Cole was less amused.

He read the leaked rules twice, jaw tightening.

“This is culty.”

Tomas nodded.

“Yeah.”

Alina leaned over.

“But they’re using the same phrases.”

“That’s the problem,” Tomas said.

Reyes pointed a sauce spoon at him.

“So your haunted book got bootlegged by weirdos.”

“Basically.”

Cole wiped his hands on a towel.

“This is why policies need managers.”

Everyone looked at him.

He frowned.

“What?”

Tomas said, “Nothing. That was actually chapter relevant.”

Cole said, “I don’t want to be chapter relevant. I want ranch cups off expo.”

Then he walked away.

But Tomas watched him stop at the schedule board.

The official INTERVENTION patch note from Chapter Six was still taped there.

**Attention magnifies ripple.**

**Ripple is not authority.**

**Authority is not wisdom.**

**Base reality has priority.**

Cole took it down.

For a second, Tomas panicked.

Then Cole grabbed a marker and wrote beneath it:

**If this ever makes the kitchen worse, it comes down. People first.**

He taped it back up.

The app buzzed in Tomas’s pocket.

**Anti-cage artifact detected.**

Tomas smiled.

Cole had no idea.

Or maybe he had every idea.

---

The official statement dropped at noon.

Every INTERVENTION screen went black.

Phones.

Books.

XP3000 units.

Archive pages.

Even Tomas’s app, mid-dish shift, shut itself down and displayed only white text.

No logo.

No music.

No animation.

Just:

**SYSTEM-WIDE ETHICAL NOTICE**

Then:

**INTERVENTION is not a religion.**

**INTERVENTION is not therapy.**

**INTERVENTION is not a replacement for family, community, medical care, crisis support, school, work, or direct human accountability.**

**INTERVENTION language must not be used to isolate, coerce, shame, recruit, diagnose, or control.**

**No player has authority over another player’s reality.**

**No module outranks consent.**

**No prompt outranks safety.**

**No story outranks the person living it.**

**Base reality has priority.**

The kitchen got quiet.

Not totally quiet.

The fryer still popped.

The printer still printed.

But everyone was reading.

Miles said, “That last part hits.”

Alina nodded.

“No story outranks the person living it.”

Reyes looked at Tomas.

“That means you can’t tell me I’m in Yelp ghost reality anymore?”

Tomas said, “I can still suggest it.”

Cole said from expo, “And I can still tell you to stop reading reviews before rush because base reality says it makes you annoying.”

Reyes pointed at him.

“That’s fair leadership.”

The screen continued:

**If a group, creator, influencer, teacher, partner, parent, employer, or player uses INTERVENTION language to reduce your agency, isolate you from support, or make themselves the only interpreter of your experience, leave that structure when safe and seek real-world support.**

Then the final line appeared.

**Do not build cages and call them worlds.**

No one joked.

Not immediately.

Even Reyes let that one breathe.

Then Cole said, softly, “Yeah.”

Just yeah.

Like Tomas’s boss had said at table nine.

Like the sentence had reached something older than the game.

---

The True Observers responded within an hour.

Of course they did.

Their leader posted a video wearing a white hoodie with **BASE REALITY** embroidered across the chest.

He was handsome in the way algorithms liked.

Calm voice.

Soft lighting.

Bookshelf background.

A plant that had probably never known struggle.

He said the official statement proved INTERVENTION had been compromised.

He said the developers were afraid of “true perception.”

He said base reality was only for beginners.

He said advanced observers understood that some people needed to be “guided out of false rooms.”

Tomas watched thirty seconds before the app froze the video.

**Cage language detected.**

He muttered, “No kidding.”

The app highlighted phrases:

**true perception**

**advanced observers**

**guided**

**false rooms**

**compromised**

Then displayed:

**Cage systems often create hierarchy around interpretation.**

Tomas typed:

He sounds convincing.

**Yes.**

That answer scared him.

The app continued:

**Dangerous systems are more effective when they borrow true language.**

Tomas thought of the conspiracy video from Chapter Nine.

True fragments.

False architecture.

The man in the hoodie continued:

“Some people will call this control because they fear transformation.”

The app froze the video again.

**Reframe of boundary as fear.**

The video resumed.

“Your family may resist your awakening.”

Freeze.

**Pre-isolation narrative.**

Resume.

“Only those who have truly entered the deeper rooms will understand.”

Freeze.

**In-group superiority.**

Tomas closed the video.

He felt dirty.

Not because the man was obviously evil.

Because he was almost right in enough places to be dangerous.

That was how cages worked.

They did not begin by saying, “Enter prison.”

They began by saying, “You are finally understood.”

---

That night, Mara Vale went live.

Not planned.

No announcement.

No glam.

Just Mara in a hoodie, sitting on a studio floor with a piano behind her.

Millions joined within minutes.

She looked tired.

Angry.

Human.

“I wasn’t going to say anything,” she said. “Because I’m not the spokesperson for INTERVENTION. I’m not a developer. I’m not a teacher. I’m not a therapist. But I am someone whose accidental post helped make this bigger, so I’m going to be very clear.”

The chat flooded.

She ignored it.

“If anyone uses these ideas to tell you they are the only one who understands your reality, that is not awakening. That is control.”

Tomas watched from the apartment table with Maribel standing behind him.

Eli was asleep.

The paper doors rustled on the wall.

Mara continued.

“If someone says your family or friends are false rooms just because they question the group, be careful. If someone tells you not to ask for outside help, be careful. If someone says your resistance means you’re not evolved enough, be careful.”

She leaned closer.

“The point of noticing layers is not to give one person power over all the layers.”

The comments slowed.

For once, people seemed to be listening more than performing.

Mara said:

“The most important rule is not mystical. It is not aesthetic. It is not lore. It is this: if the framework makes you less free, less connected, less safe, or less able to question the person explaining it, get out when you can.”

She paused.

Then:

“Do not build cages and call them worlds.”

She ended the live.

No song.

No promo.

No link.

Just that.

Maribel crossed her arms.

“I like her.”

Tomas nodded.

“Yeah.”

“She looks tired.”

“Yeah.”

“Fame seems awful.”

“Yeah.”

The app buzzed.

**High-radius anti-cage intervention detected.**

Tomas looked at it.

Then at Mara’s frozen face on the screen.

He wondered how heavy it must be to speak carefully because millions of people might build rooms out of your words.

He did not envy her.

Not even a little.

---

The True Observers began falling apart three days later.

Not completely.

Cages rarely collapsed all at once.

But cracks appeared.

Members started posting screenshots.

Rules.

Private messages.

Voice notes.

One former member said they had joined because the language helped them feel seen.

Then slowly, questioning the leader became “resisting the deeper room.”

Calling family became “returning to old architecture.”

Taking a break became “base reality regression.”

Everything useful had been inverted.

Trigger language became obedience language.

Ripple language became pressure.

Room language became isolation.

Base reality became whatever the leader said it was.

The official INTERVENTION archive created a new public category:

**Cage Events**

Not to shame victims.

To map patterns.

Each case had the same structure:

**Useful language enters vulnerable room.**

**Authority claims special interpretation.**

**Outside support is downgraded.**

**Doubt is reframed as proof of resistance.**

**Identity becomes dependent on group.**

**Exit becomes emotionally dangerous.**

At the bottom of every case:

**If you are reading this and recognizing your room, tell a real person outside the room when safe.**

Tomas read those archives in pieces.

He had to stop often.

Not because they were unfamiliar.

Because they were too familiar.

Not just from groups.

From relationships.

Jobs.

Families.

Addiction.

Old friendships.

Even his own mind.

Anything could become a cage if leaving it felt like losing the only version of yourself you knew how to be.

---

At Wing Kings, the anti-cage rule became unexpectedly useful.

Not with cults.

With closing duties.

Reyes tried to declare that because Tomas was “dish architecture,” all pans belonged to him spiritually.

Tomas pointed at Cole’s sign.

“If this framework makes me less free, I’m leaving the structure.”

Alina laughed so hard she had to put down a sauce bottle.

Cole yelled from expo, “Stop weaponizing ethics and run the pans.”

Tomas said, “Base reality has priority.”

Cole said, “Base reality says pans.”

Fair.

But later that night, the rule mattered for real.

Miles came back from a hospital visit with Andre and looked wrecked.

He started apologizing to everyone.

For being slow.

For leaving early last week.

For talking about his brother too much.

For not being fun.

For existing with needs.

Reyes started to say, “Bro, you’re good.”

But Tomas saw the loop.

Miles was trying to make himself small enough not to burden the room.

A cage built out of gratitude.

Tomas said, “Hey.”

Miles stopped.

“You don’t have to pay rent on support by apologizing every five minutes.”

Miles stared at him.

Reyes pointed.

“That one.”

Alina nodded.

“Yeah.”

Cole, without looking up from expo, said, “Correct. Also, table eleven needs drinks.”

Everybody moved.

But Miles stopped apologizing for the rest of the hour.

Anti-cage intervention.

Small version.

Correct surface.

---

Chapter Fourteen completed late.

Tomas opened the book after work, sitting on the apartment floor while Eli slept and Maribel watched a crime documentary too quietly.

The final page read:

**The opposite of a cage is not a door.**

Tomas frowned.

The page continued:

**A door can still lead to another cage.**

**The opposite of a cage is agency with support.**

He read that again.

Agency with support.

Not independence as loneliness.

Not support as control.

Both.

The page continued:

**A healthy world allows questions.**

**A healthy framework survives correction.**

**A healthy leader does not need to become the only door.**

**A healthy story returns the reader to their own life with more options, not fewer.**

Then:

**Do not build cages and call them worlds.**

**Do not build mirrors and demand worship.**

**Do not build doors that only open back to you.**

Tomas sat very still.

That one was not just for cult leaders.

It was for everyone.

Parents.

Teachers.

Managers.

Artists.

Partners.

Friends.

Sponsors.

Creators.

Him.

Especially him, maybe.

Every time someone trusted you, there was a tiny temptation to become necessary.

To become the expert.

To become the only one who understood.

To become the door.

The book did not let him have that.

Good.

At the bottom of the page, Chapter Fifteen unlocked.

The final chapter.

The title appeared slowly.

**After You Close the Book**

Tomas’s chest tightened.

The app displayed one last system message.

**Final module will not run inside the book.**

He whispered, “What does that mean?”

The app answered:

**Correct question.**

Then the book closed itself.

Not dramatically.

No slam.

Just a soft fall of pages.

The cover returned to its original sentence.

**The game begins after you close the book.**

Tomas looked around the apartment.

The crooked table.

The couch.

The paper doors.

Maribel asleep now, crime documentary still whispering judgment from the TV.

Eli’s yellow keys taped to the wall.

His phone.

His shoes.

His life.

The book was done teaching for the night.

Base reality waited.

And for the first time, Tomas understood that maybe INTERVENTION had never wanted to be carried forever.

Maybe it had only been trying to teach him how to walk without holding it.
